\section{Supplementary Results}

This SI reports supplementary results that support the main manuscript, with emphasis on dimensional scaling and the distribution of regional heterogeneity.

\subsection{S1. Dimensional scaling and the information-sparsity boundary}

All four reported person-level configurations with $K\leq256$ show a diffusion advantage over IPF in the main text. To test whether this advantage persists at higher dimensionality, we additionally evaluate the fine-grained 5-variable setting with $K=512$ ($4\times2\times4\times4\times4$ bins). At this resolution, diffusion TVD reaches 0.139 while IPF achieves 0.112, yielding a reversal of $+0.028$ (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:S1_Kscale}).

Two mechanisms drive this reversal. First, cell sparsity becomes severe: at $K=32$, each joint cell contains roughly 220 persons on average, whereas at $K=512$ the average falls to about 14. Second, the conditioning inputs become much less informative relative to the target joint: the pairwise condition vector provides 128 entries, while the $K=512$ joint has 511 free parameters. Extending training from 10k to 30k epochs reduces TVD from 0.182 to 0.147, but does not reach IPF's 0.112, indicating that the bottleneck is information rather than optimization alone. Pairwise conditioning at $K=512$ is also worse than marginal-only conditioning (0.249 vs.\ 0.147), consistent with a setting in which the available pairwise statistics are no longer sufficient to support accurate recovery of the full joint.

\subsection{S2. Distribution of regional dependence heterogeneity}

Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:S2_heterogeneity} expands the heterogeneity analysis behind Fig.~1 in the main text. Across all 2,456 PUMAs, TVD-to-global has a mean of 0.191 and a long right tail, indicating that a substantial minority of regions deviate strongly from the national-average dependence structure. Michigan's 68 PUMAs span a comparable range of values, supporting its use as a held-out test state for evaluating geographic generalization.
